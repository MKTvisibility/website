What customers need to find before they choose you

MKT Visibility · October 27, 2026

A customer hears about your shop from a friend. Later, they search your name, see a social page, and check whether you are open. The page lists one set of hours, the website lists another, and the most recent promotion ended months ago. They may still like your business. They simply do not have enough confidence to plan a visit.

Think in customer decisions

Ask what a person needs to decide next. A restaurant customer may need the menu, location, hours, and reservation details. A home-service customer may need service boundaries, evidence of relevant work, and a way to request an estimate. An artist's customer may need to understand dimensions, availability, and pickup or shipping.

These are different information needs. That is why a useful marketing plan starts with the business and customer rather than a list of platforms to fill.

Google advises eligible businesses to keep their profile information complete and accurate, including hours and categories. Source: Google local-ranking guidance. Our practical recommendation is to extend the same accuracy check across your website, social profiles, and community listings.

How MKT approaches the work

A visibility review should follow the customer's route: search the business, open its profiles, visit the website, and try the next step. Record each issue with its location, business impact, and recommended correction. Separate urgent obstacles, such as a broken contact form, from improvements that can wait.

This gives the owner a useful order of work. It also makes a later report meaningful: we can show what was corrected and what still needs attention. Specific checks and fixes depend on the agreed service scope.

Try it yourself

Before you search, remember that your own device may give you a familiar view of your business. Your browser can suggest websites from your browsing history as you type, and Google may personalize some search results using past activity and account settings. If you repeatedly search for or visit your own website, what you see may differ from what a first-time customer sees. A familiar site appearing near the top for you does not establish that it appears in the same position for everyone.

There are two different things to notice: the suggestions that appear beneath the browser’s address bar before you submit a search, and the results page the search engine displays afterward. A history-based shortcut to your website is not evidence of its search ranking. Repeated visits do not automatically move your website higher in everyone else’s results. Sources: Google on differing search results and Chrome on address-bar suggestions.

For a useful comparison, open a fresh private or incognito window, stay signed out of the search engine, and submit the full search instead of selecting a remembered website suggestion. This helps reduce the influence of existing account and browser history, but it does not create a completely neutral result: location, language, device, and activity within that private session can still matter. Ask someone who has not previously searched for or visited your website to try the same search on their own device. Compare the search engine, exact wording, location, and date rather than treating one result as a universal position.

Try both your business name and a service-plus-location search, such as “custom cakes in Longmont,” using a phrase relevant to your business. Being found by name is a different test from being discovered by someone who does not know your name yet.

Then open the relevant pages on your phone and answer five questions: What do you offer? Who is it for? Where or how do you serve customers? When are you available? What should I do next? Write down missing or contradictory information and ask the unfamiliar visitor to repeat that exercise too.

Where professional help adds value

The research is only the beginning. Someone must obtain access, confirm the correct facts, make changes, test them, and revisit information when the business changes. MKT can help coordinate those tasks so the information customers find supports the experience you want them to have.
