Decide what to do yourself and what to delegate

MKT Visibility · October 30, 2026

Knowing how marketing works gives you choices. You might manage it yourself, keep part of the work in-house, or hire a partner to coordinate a larger share. The right choice depends on capacity, skill, business priorities, and the cost of work left undone.

The purpose of this library is to help you make that choice with a clearer understanding of what the work involves.

Count the complete workload

For one month, record time spent gathering facts, taking photographs, writing, designing, checking rights, approving, scheduling, responding, updating pages, and reviewing results. Include the time spent returning to a task after an interruption.

Then identify the cost of neglected work. Are customers seeing old information? Are inquiries waiting? Is the owner postponing a valuable service because marketing is unfinished? Treat these as business questions rather than assuming every missed post represents a lost sale.

Build a practical division of responsibility

The owner is usually best placed to confirm services, availability, policies, and customer commitments. A marketing partner can organize the calendar, create approved material, coordinate publication, maintain agreed information, and explain the evidence.

Write down what stays with the business and what is delegated. For each task, identify the person doing the work, the approver, and the deadline. A useful agreement also explains access, included channels, response coverage, content quantities, revisions, reporting, and separately priced work.

Compare costs honestly

Suppose an owner spends eight hours per month on marketing and assigns that time a planning value of $40 per hour. The estimated time value is $320. This is a hypothetical decision aid, not automatic cash savings or proof that any service fee pays for itself.

Compare that estimate with the actual proposed scope, your ability to use the freed time productively, any remaining owner work, and the full costs of tools, content production, advertising, or travel. A fair comparison uses comparable tasks.

Create your next 30-day plan

In week one, confirm your main goal, current information, and working inquiry route. In week two, collect common customer questions and prepare a small content batch. In week three, publish at a sustainable pace and monitor responses. In week four, review the work and evidence, correct problems, and decide what to repeat.

You do not need to implement every lesson at once. Choose the work most likely to remove a current customer obstacle or support a specific business priority.
