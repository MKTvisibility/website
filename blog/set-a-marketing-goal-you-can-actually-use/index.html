Set a marketing goal you can actually use

MKT Visibility · October 27, 2026

“We need to post more” describes an activity. It does not explain what the business needs that activity to accomplish. Before you commit time to marketing, decide which business result deserves attention.

Imagine a Longmont repair business with open appointment slots on Tuesdays. Its priority could be more qualified inquiries for Tuesday service, rather than a larger audience everywhere. That choice affects the message, the geographic focus, the landing page, and the information the owner tracks.

Write one short goal statement

Use this planning format: “During this period, we want this customer group to take this action, and we will measure it this way.” For the repair business, a hypothetical goal is eight qualified Tuesday-service inquiries during the next month, recorded in a shared inquiry log. This is a planning target, not a prediction.

A qualified inquiry needs a definition. It might mean a person within the service area who needs a service you offer and has provided a usable way to respond. A message asking for a service you do not provide should not inflate the count.

Measure both the result and its early signals

The final result might be completed bookings. Earlier signals could include relevant website visits, form starts, and qualified requests. Those signals help explain where the process is working or failing. High reach with few relevant visits may suggest a weak connection between message and audience. Several requests with no bookings may point to availability, pricing, response time, or customer fit.

These are questions to investigate, not conclusions the numbers prove automatically. Small samples can change sharply from week to week.

How MKT approaches the work

Before building a calendar, define the goal, starting point, capacity, offer, and method of measurement with the owner. Choose one primary result and a few supporting indicators. Agree on what counts before the campaign starts so a report does not quietly change the definition of success afterward.

MKT's recommendation is to review the information regularly and change the plan when the evidence or business need changes. More activity is not automatically a better plan.

Try it yourself

Finish the goal statement for your business. Then ask: Can we handle the result if the campaign works? Who responds? What information will we record? What would cause us to pause or revise the promotion? If you cannot answer those questions, resolve them before publishing.

Where professional help adds value

An outside marketing partner can translate a broad wish into a clear campaign, keep the message aligned with the goal, and distinguish activity from business outcomes. You still provide the business judgment: which services matter, what customers need, and how much work you can deliver.

A useful goal tells you what to do, what to measure, and what to stop doing.
